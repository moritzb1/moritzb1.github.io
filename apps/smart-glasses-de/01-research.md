# Market Research Report: Smart Glasses in Germany

*Prepared by: Research Agent | Date: 2026-11-20 | Scope: consumer and enterprise smart/AI/AR glasses, German market*

> Note on data quality: Official German unit sales figures for smart glasses could not be found. Figures below are global vendor data, survey data, or third-party forecasts and are labelled as such.

## 1. Market Trends Summary

**Strong momentum, led by camera/audio "AI glasses" without displays.**
- EssilorLuxottica (Meta's eyewear partner) sold **more than 7 million smart glasses globally in 2025**, more than triple 2024 ([UploadVR](https://uploadvr.com/meta-essilorluxottica-sold-7-million-smart-glasses-in-2025/), [heise](https://heise.de/-11173680)).
- In Q3 2026 its wearables revenue grew **+20 %** (about **€486 m**, roughly 6.5 % of group sales). That is a clear slowdown from the near-doubling in Q2 2026 ([mixed-news](https://mixed-news.com/en/essilorluxottica-q3-2026-wearables-revenue-up-20-percent/), [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/dtc-and-wearables-propel-essilorluxottica-to-7-49-billion-euros-in-q3/2026102175293)). The €486 m figure is secondary reporting.
- IDC: global XR device shipments grew **44.4 % in 2025**, driven by smart glasses. IDC forecasts **+33.5 % in 2026**, mostly from glasses *without* displays ([IDC, Mar 2026](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/press-releases/xr-market-grew-44-4-in-2025-as-smart-glasses-redefine-the-category/)). In Q3 2025, **Western Europe was the fastest-growing region at +106.5 % YoY** (IDC smart glasses tracker, via [IDC](https://www.idc.com/promo/arvr)). Meanwhile, VR/MR *headsets* in Western Europe declined about 25 %.
- Germany market size: MarketsandMarkets puts it at **USD 68 m (2024) → USD 299 m (2029)**. This is a **third-party estimate**, not measured sales ([MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/geography/smart-glasses-market/Germany)). Retailer Galaxus reports smart glasses sales **up about 4x YoY** in 2026, but that figure covers one retailer only ([Galaxus/digitec](https://www.digitec.ch/en/page/plenty-of-critics-still-a-success-sales-of-smart-glasses-have-quadrupled-43954)).
- **Display glasses are arriving in Germany.** Meta Ray-Ban Display launched in Germany on 13 Oct 2026 at **€899**, sold via Meta, Telekom shops and MediaMarkt ([Telekom](https://www.telekom.com/en/newsroom/latest-updates/media-information/2026/10/launch-telekom-offers-meta-ray-ban-display), [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.com/Meta-Ray-Ban-Display-endlich-auch-in-Deutschland-verfuegbar.1407288.0.html)). Google announced Android XR glasses with Warby Parker and Gentle Monster for fall 2026 in "select markets" ([MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/05/19/samsung-reveals-warby-parker-gentle-monster-android-xr-smartglasses/)). **No confirmed German launch was found.**

**German consumer attitude is split, and privacy is the defining issue.** A Bitkom survey (n = 1,164, May–June 2026) found:
- **43 %** are interested in wearing smart glasses (15 % have already used them).
- **52 %** rule them out.
- **76 %** do not want to be recorded.
- **62 %** would not let a wearer into their home.
- **82 %** want clear recording indicators.

Source: [Bitkom, 21 Aug 2026](https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/An-Smart-Glasses-scheiden-sich-die-Geister).

**Regulation is tightening.**
- **Possible ban debate (2026):** Hamburg's data protection commissioner called a German sales ban of Meta glasses possible. His argument is that they may be a "disguised recording device" under §8 TDDDG. The **Bundesnetzagentur saw no grounds for a ban** and treats the recording LED as sufficient. Politicians and HateAid continue to push for a ban, and HateAid has filed criminal complaints against sellers including Apollo-Optik, MediaMarkt and Mister Spex ([CORRECTIV](https://correctiv.org/aktuelles/kuenstliche-intelligenz/2026/08/27/debatte-um-smart-glasses-ist-das-kleine-licht-bei-aufnahmen-erkennbar/), [deskmodder](https://www.deskmodder.de/blog/2026/08/15/meta-smart-glasses-bundesnetzagentur-sieht-derzeit-keinen-grund-fuer-ein-verbot/), [t3n](https://t3n.de/news/meta-smart-glasses-verkaufsverbot-deutschland-datenschutz-1755959/), [GIGA](https://www.giga.de/tech/strafanzeige-gegen-fielmann-diese-brille-bringt-den-optiker-in-schwierigkeiten--01KZTSH6STBR64M6MY4ZB2JS5Q)).
- **EDPB statement:** On 13 Oct 2026 the EDPB adopted Statement 01/2026 on smart glasses. It is an initial statement that flags bystander and background-audio risks ([EDPB PDF](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-10/20261310_edpb_statement_smart-glasses_en.pdf), [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-data/edpb-releases-smart-glasses-statement)).
- **EU AI Act:** Article 5 prohibitions (in force since Feb 2025) include untargeted facial-image scraping and certain real-time biometric identification. **No glasses-specific AI Act provision was found**, but any face-recognition feature is high-risk territory ([Bundesnetzagentur](https://bundesnetzagentur.de/EN/Areas/Digitalisation/AI/08_ProhibitedPractices/start.html)).
- **Workplace use:** Glasses that can monitor behaviour or performance are "technical devices" under **§87(1) No. 6 BetrVG**, so the works council must co-determine their use, normally through a works agreement ([Kliemt, Sep 2026](https://www.kliemt.blog/2026/09/21/wenn-die-brille-zur-kamera-wird-smart-glasses-am-arbeitsplatz/)).

## 3. Target Customer Profiles

*Profiles are synthesized from the cited sources. Segment sizes are **estimates**; no German segmentation data was found.*

1. **Tech-savvy early adopters and content creators (urban, 16–39).**
   - They want hands-free capture, an AI assistant and music.
   - **Pain points:** social stigma and limited battery life.
   - **Criteria:** design/brand, camera quality, price under €500.
   - Interest is fairly evenly spread across ages; even young Germans reject being filmed (71 % of 16–29-year-olds) ([Bitkom](https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/An-Smart-Glasses-scheiden-sich-die-Geister)).
2. **Glasses wearers and professionals who need a discreet display (30–60).**
   - They want notifications, teleprompter, translation and navigation without pulling out the phone.
   - **Criteria:** prescription lenses, weight and look, no camera (social acceptance).
   - This is Even Realities' positioning.
   - Germany has a large optician infrastructure (Fielmann alone has about 615 German stores per [drohnen.de](https://www.drohnen.de/89054/fielmann-smart-glasses/), store count undated).
3. **Sports and outdoor users.**
   - Use cases are POV video and fitness data, served by Oakley Meta Vanguard.
4. **Enterprise and industry (logistics, maintenance, field service, healthcare).**
   - Use cases are pick-by-vision and remote expert support. Example: Fiege logistics in Worms uses data glasses with works council support ([betriebsrat.de/Böckler sources via Kliemt](https://www.kliemt.blog/2026/09/21/wenn-die-brille-zur-kamera-wird-smart-glasses-am-arbeitsplatz/)).
   - **Criteria:** GDPR/BetrVG compliance, robustness, device management.
5. **Accessibility users (low vision, hearing).**
   - Uses include AI scene description and live captions. EssilorLuxottica's Nuance Audio hearing glasses point to this direction.
   - No German market data was found.

## 4. Barriers and Opportunities for Market Entry

**Barriers**
- **Privacy and legal risk is the main barrier in Germany.** A camera device risks being classed as a "disguised recording device" (§8 TDDDG). It faces GDPR scrutiny (EDPB 01/2026), criminal complaints against retailers, and strong public rejection (76 % do not want to be filmed).
- **Certification.** CE marking under the Radio Equipment Directive is required. Since **1 Aug 2025** this includes the RED cybersecurity delegated act (harmonized standards **EN 18031-1/-2/-3**), and without compliance there is no CE mark and no EU sale ([heise](https://heise.de/-10268367), [BSI](https://www.bsigroup.com/en-IE/products-and-services/assessment-and-certification/product-testing-certification/radio-equipment-directive-cybersecurity-testing-for-radio-products/)). Other likely requirements are GPSR, WEEE/battery rules and AI Act transparency. These are listed from general EU law; the details still need legal verification.
- **Prescription logistics.** Meta's warranty applies only if prescription lenses come from certified sellers ([Meta](https://www.meta.com/de/ai-glasses/prescription/)). Opticians are cautious: Fielmann carries smart glasses in only about 200 of about 1,300 stores and calls them a niche ([drweb.de](https://www.drweb.de/smartglasses-fielmann-nische-filialen/), [CIO](https://www.cio.de/article/4204355/smartglasses-fielmann-zerlegt-metas-traum-vom-smartphone-aus.html)).
- **Incumbent scale.** Meta plus EssilorLuxottica control brands, lens labs and retail.
- **B2B friction.** Works-council co-determination lengthens enterprise sales cycles.

**Opportunities**
- **"Privacy-by-design" positioning.** Options include camera-free products or a clearly visible, tamper-proof recording indicator, on-device AI and EU data hosting. This answers the 82 % who demand recording indicators and the regulatory pressure on Meta. Even Realities and Brilliant Labs show the positioning is viable.
- **Optician channel.** Apollo, Fielmann and independents want a credible product with prescription integration. A product that is not legally contested could win shelf space that Meta products currently put at risk.
- **Carrier and electronics retail.** Telekom and MediaMarkt already sell smart glasses with financing (Telekom: €22.10 per month).
- **German localization.** German-language AI, DSGVO documentation and a GDPR-compliant works-agreement template for B2B customers.
- **Timing.** Western Europe is growing fastest (IDC). Google and Samsung glasses are not yet confirmed for Germany, which leaves a window in the €400–700 "everyday display glasses" segment.
