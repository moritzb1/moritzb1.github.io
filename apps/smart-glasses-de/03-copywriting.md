### Core Benefits

**No camera. Nothing to explain.**
Vistrel has no camera module, not even a switched-off one. A clear "no camera" mark on the frame shows the people around you at a glance. In Germany, 76 % of people do not want to be recorded (Bitkom 2026). With Vistrel, you never become the reason for that worry, whether you are at dinner, in a meeting or visiting friends at home.

**Your prescription, your optician, from day one.**
Vistrel is planned with prescription lenses fitted in-store by your own optician from launch. You get an eye test, a proper fit and after-sales care from the people who already look after your eyesight. They are your glasses first and smart glasses second.

**The information you need, right where you look.**
A small heads-up display in one lens shows what matters: the next turn on your route, a message you were waiting for, a live translation, or your speaker notes during a presentation. Your phone stays in your pocket and your attention stays with the people in front of you.

**German-speaking, EU-hosted, listening only when you ask.**
The assistant understands German and processes on the device wherever possible. Anything that goes to the cloud is hosted in the EU, and we put that promise in writing. The microphone uses push-to-talk by default, and a visible indicator shows when it is listening. You keep control, and everyone around you can see it.

## Pitch Deck Key Points

1. **Problem:** Germans want the convenience of smart glasses but not the camera. 76 % do not want to be recorded, 82 % want clear recording indicators, and 62 % would not let a wearer into their home (Bitkom 2026).
2. **Problem:** Camera glasses now carry legal risk. There is the §8 TDDDG ban debate, HateAid has filed criminal complaints against retailers, and the EDPB issued Statement 01/2026. Retailers and opticians need a product they can sell with confidence.
3. **Solution:** Vistrel is a pair of camera-free display glasses for glasses wearers and professionals aged 30–60. It shows notifications, navigation, translation and teleprompter text, and it is fitted by the optician with prescription lenses from launch.
4. **Differentiation vs. Meta:** Meta Ray-Ban Display costs €899 and offers prescription lenses in Germany only "later". Vistrel's planned target price is €599, with prescription at launch and no camera.
5. **Differentiation vs. Even Realities:** our closest rival is also camera-free, at €699 plus €169 for prescription. Vistrel plans to be €100 lower on the frame and display, German first (German AI, German support, DSGVO documents), and to put EU data hosting in writing.
6. **Benefit for retailers:** with no camera, Vistrel stays out of the ban debate. Opticians get a fitting guide, a demo unit and a one-page legal Q&A, and the product brings glasses wearers back into their stores.
7. **Go-to-market:** we start with 15–25 independent opticians and one chain pilot in 2–3 cities, then expand nationally using real pilot sales data. B2B pilots in logistics and field service are planned from month 18.
8. **The window is open now:** Google/Samsung glasses are not yet confirmed for Germany. Vistrel aims to win optician loyalty in the €400–700 everyday display segment before they arrive.
